# Ingestion and event resolution for Yggdrasil: GDELT, deterministic dedup, story/event detection, narrative formation

Scope: Engine 1 Part 1 (source adapters, then observation envelope, then deterministic dedup, then canonical observations, then an immutable archive plus a live stream) and the next layer up (soft membership in evolving narratives, levels of detail, attention mass per window for a Hawkes-type model). Current date 2026-10-03. Years are flagged. "MEASURED" marks numbers this researcher produced on 2026-10-03 by downloading and parsing all 96 GDELT 2.0 English batches for 2025-01-27 (GKG, Events export, Mentions) on the target-class machine (4 cores, 15 GB RAM, Python 3.12 + polars 1.44). Method details are under Key Question 8.

## 1. GDELT 2.0 Events and GKG: fields, cadence, data-quality problems, validation studies, finance uses

### Takeaway
GDELT 2.0 is a 15-minute, machine-coded, append-only feed with three English tables (Events, Mentions, GKG) and a separate Translingual stream that is larger than the English one. It has no per-article publication timestamp and does not deduplicate GKG documents. Its event coding has well-documented validity problems: many false-positive events, residual duplicate events, geographic and capital bias, and coverage that drifts over time. So Yggdrasil should treat GDELT as a high-recall, low-precision sensor. Use it for attention counts (documents per narrative per window), not as ground truth about events, and derive independence weights from URLs and sources rather than from CAMEO events.

### Cited Findings
**Cadence, files, identifiers**
- GDELT 2.0 produces Event and Mentions tables "every 15 minutes"; files carry a ".csv" extension but are tab-delimited; GDELT 2.0 event files begin February 19, 2015 — [GDELT Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- GlobalEventID is "globally unique" but "NOT always" sequential with date and "should NOT be used to sort events by date". The ID sequence was reset higher at the 2015-02-18/19 switch to GDELT 2.0 — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- A small number of early records reportedly got duplicate GlobalEventID values "due to an unknown error in the numbering subsystem", described as "safe to discard or renumber". This was seen only via a search snippet of the GDELT 1.0 data format codebook, not verified in full text — [GDELT Data Format Codebook (1.0)](https://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Data_Format_Codebook.pdf)
- GKGRECORDID has the form "YYYYMMDDHHMMSS-X" (or "-TX" for documents translated by GDELT Translingual), i.e., batch timestamp plus a within-batch serial, and must be treated as a string — [GKG Codebook V2.1](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Global_Knowledge_Graph_Codebook-V2.1.pdf)
- The update manifests (lastupdate.txt, lastupdate-translation.txt, masterfilelist.txt) list each file as "size md5 url". On 2026-10-03 at 19:00 UTC the feed was still live and the translation manifest was one batch (15 min) behind the English one — [GDELT lastupdate.txt](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/lastupdate.txt); [lastupdate-translation.txt](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/lastupdate-translation.txt); [masterfilelist.txt](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/masterfilelist.txt)

**Event fields that matter**
- Day is the date the event took place (daily resolution). DATEADDED (YYYYMMDDHHMMSS, UTC) is the 15-minute batch in which it was added and "is the field that should be used" for 15-minute work — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- EventCode, EventBaseCode, and EventRootCode follow the three-level CAMEO taxonomy and must be stored as strings because codes can have leading zeros. QuadClass is 1=Verbal Cooperation, 2=Material Cooperation, 3=Verbal Conflict, 4=Material Conflict. GoldsteinScale (-10 to +10) depends on event type, not magnitude ("two riots, one with 10 people and one with 10,000, will both receive the same Goldstein score") — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- NumMentions, NumSources, NumArticles, and AvgTone are computed only "during the 15 minute update in which it was first seen". They are "not updated" later and are kept "for legacy purposes – the new Mentions table should be used instead". NumMentions also counts multiple references within one document and is a composite with reprocessed-article mentions. IsRootEvent likewise reflects only the first report — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- SOURCEURL is "the first news report it found this event in"… "this may not always be the very first report, but is at least in the first few reports" — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- Geography: fulltext geocoding with automatic disambiguation. Geo_Type distinguishes country, US state, US city, world city, and world state. Geo_FeatureID (GNS/GNIS) resolves alternative spellings. Country- and state-level matches are placed at the centroid with a blank FeatureID — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)

**Mentions table**
- The Mentions table has one row per mention of an event in an article (an event mentioned in 100 articles appears 100 times). Mentions are recorded "irrespective of the date of the original event". EventTimeDate is the original DATEADDED and MentionTimeDate is the current batch. MentionType 1=WEB, 2=CITATIONONLY, 3=CORE, 4=DTIC, 5=JSTOR, 6=NONTEXTUALSOURCE. Other fields: SentenceID, Actor1/Actor2/Action CharOffset, InRawText (1 = found in unaltered raw text), Confidence (10-100%, "how aggressively GDELT had to perform tasks like coreference or grammatical restructuring"), MentionDocLen, MentionDocTone, and MentionDocTranslationInfo (srclc and engine) — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)
- The codebook warns that "Machine translation is often not as accurate as human translation". Users requiring the highest confidence "may wish to exclude events whose only mentions are in translated reports" — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)

**GKG 2.1 fields and dedup behavior**
- GKG 2.1 "no longer clusters documents together based on shared metadata – if 20 articles all contain the same list of extracted locations, themes, people, and organizations, they will appear as 20 separate entries". GKG 1.0 did cluster (NumArts). GKG 2.1 also includes any article with "ANY successfully extracted information, INCLUDING GCAM emotional scores", even with no geography — [GKG Codebook V2.1](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Global_Knowledge_Graph_Codebook-V2.1.pdf)
- V2.1DATE is "the same for all rows in a file", i.e., the batch timestamp. It is not a per-article publication time, although the codebook frames it as publication date — [GKG Codebook V2.1](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Global_Knowledge_Graph_Codebook-V2.1.pdf)
- V1.5TONE is a comma list (Tone, Positive, Negative, Polarity, Activity Reference Density, Self/Group Reference Density, Word Count). GCAM has "over 2,300 dimensions" from 24 packages, with native emotion dictionaries in 15 languages. Keys are "DictionaryID.DimensionID", and zero dimensions are omitted. GDELT Translingual translates "65 languages" in real time — [GKG Codebook V2.1](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Global_Knowledge_Graph_Codebook-V2.1.pdf)

**Validation studies and critiques**
- Ward et al. (2013), comparing GDELT and ICEWS: "both databases pick up major events remarkably well", but GDELT volume is far larger and grows with an exponentially increasing media corpus. ICEWS aims at a ground-truth chronology, while GDELT catalogues all media reports — [Predictive Heuristics blog summary of Ward et al. 2013](https://blog.predictiveheuristics.com/2013/10/17/gdelt-and-icews-a-short-comparison/)
- Hammond & Weidmann (2014, Research & Politics), comparing against two hand-coded conflict datasets: GDELT's correlation with them is "mediocre". At the local level there are major geographic-bias issues, and the authors advise caution for subnational geo-spatial analysis — [KOPS Konstanz record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/cd1512fc-1853-4302-9829-cb83f47cbbcb)
- Kwak & An (2014), studying 195,000 disasters from April 2013 to July 2014 across more than 10,000 outlets: strong regionalism in news geography. Population, political stability, and damage drive coverage — [arXiv 1410.3710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.3710); [SMU record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/6066)

### Inferences
- Treat the GKG record (one row per document URL) as the base observation, not the Event row. Events are a derived, noisy annotation: documents that yield events carry about 7 event-mention rows each (5 new events per first-report SOURCEURL), and about 59% of documents yield no coded event at all (MEASURED). Attention per narrative should count documents and independent sources, not CAMEO events or NumMentions.
- Mapping to the four timestamps. event_time comes from Events.Day (day resolution) or from GKG V2.1EnhancedDates mentions, and is often null for GKG. published_time is not provided by GDELT: set it to null, or to an upper bound equal to the batch timestamp, and fill it from RSS pubDate or page metadata when fetched. observed_time is the GDELT batch timestamp (GKG DATE = DATEADDED = MentionTimeDate). ingested_time is Yggdrasil's clock. For deterministic replay, define ingested_time = batch_ts + a fixed, disclosed lag (for example 15 min) instead of wall clock, and keep the real wall-clock ingestion time as a separate audit field.
- Use the Mentions table, not NumMentions, for event-level attention. Filter mentions to Confidence of 70 or more or InRawText=1 for a high-precision variant, and report both variants: by the MEASURED numbers, only 23% of mentions pass Confidence of 70 or more.
- Decide and disclose English-only vs English plus Translingual. English-only drops about 74% of GDELT's documents by count (MEASURED 2.8:1 in one batch) and biases sources geographically. That compounds the Kwak & An regionalism and the Hammond & Weidmann geographic bias.
- For dedup, use GDELT IDs only as idempotency keys (GKGRECORDID, GlobalEventID plus DATEADDED). They are not content identity. The canonical document identity should be the canonical URL. Event identity should be Yggdrasil's own event cluster, not GlobalEventID, given the 13-21% residual duplicate rates.
- Store CAMEO codes as strings, and treat GoldsteinScale as a type prior, not an intensity.
- Verify each downloaded zip against the manifest md5 and store it under its SHA-256. Both the immutable archive and the replay then become content-addressed (see Key Question 7).

### Gaps
- No primary source found that quantifies GKG volume per day across 2015-2026 (source-coverage drift). Only Ward et al.'s qualitative "exponential" growth note and one MEASURED live batch.
- Full texts of the MDPI 2025 review and the 2022 Communication Methods and Measures study could not be retrieved (HTTP 403). Their numbers are from search snippets.
- No study found that validates GKG Persons, Organizations, or Themes extraction accuracy (as opposed to Event coding). No study found that quantifies machine-translation error effects on GKG themes.
- GDELT does not document its internal rule for attaching a new mention to an existing GlobalEventID vs creating a new event.

## 2. Deterministic near-duplicate and syndication detection (SimHash, MinHash/LSH, URL canonicalization, wire-copy detection, source-dependence weighting)

### Takeaway
Use a fixed, ordered cascade of exact keys: source record ID, then GUID, then canonical URL, then SHA-256 of normalized text. Add bit-reproducible near-duplicate grouping on top: 64-bit SimHash with k=3 for near-exact copies, and MinHash with 128 permutations at a fixed seed over 5-word shingles for edited syndication. Near-duplicate grouping should produce copy groups that downweight non-independent sources, not delete observations. GDELT GKG has no article text, so text-level near-duplicate detection only works for documents whose body Yggdrasil fetches (RSS, SerpApi hits). For GKG-only documents, URL and source-network rules are the only reliable deterministic tools (MEASURED: GKG metadata is far too coarse for SimHash or MinHash).

### Cited Findings
- Charikar (2002) introduced random-hyperplane hashing (simhash): for vectors u, v, Pr[h(u)=h(v)] = 1 - theta(u,v)/pi per bit — [Charikar STOC 2002](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr04/cos598B/bib/CharikarEstim.pdf)
- Manku, Jain & Das Sarma (WWW 2007): weighted features (tokenization, case folding, stop-word removal, stemming, phrase detection) feed into an f-bit simhash. "for a repository of 8B webpages, 64-bit simhash fingerprints and k = 3 are reasonable". "Choosing k = 3 is reasonable because both precision and recall are near 0.75". The paper gives permuted-table schemes for f=64, k=3 (for example "4 tables: Split 64 bits into 4 blocks, each having 16 bits", or 10, 16, or 20 tables). A batch of 1M fingerprints against 8B finishes in about 100 s on MapReduce — [Manku et al. 2007](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/33026.pdf)
- Broder (1997) defined resemblance (Jaccard of shingle sets) and containment, estimated with min-wise independent permutations on a fixed-size sample per document — [Broder 1997](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring13/cos598C/broder97resemblance.pdf)
- Indyk & Motwani (1998) introduced locality-sensitive hashing for approximate nearest neighbors — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/276698.276876)
- datasketch MinHashLSH: P(candidate | Jaccard s) = 1 - (1 - s^r)^b. The (b, r) choice is optimized to place the S-curve at the threshold while minimizing weighted false-positive and false-negative areas. With num_perm=128, threshold 0.5 gives (b=25, r=5), 0.7 gives (14, 9), and 0.9 gives (5, 25). Default num_perm is 128. The threshold is fixed at construction. Weights=(fp, fn) can be tuned — [datasketch LSH docs](https://ekzhu.com/datasketch/lsh.html)
- MEASURED: datasketch 1.x picks (b=9, r=13) for threshold 0.8 and num_perm 128. Computed recall curve: P(0.7)=0.084, P(0.8)=0.399, P(0.9)=0.929, P(0.95)=0.998. With (b=14, r=9): P(0.7)=0.438, P(0.8)=0.867, P(0.9)=0.999 — [datasketch LSH docs](https://ekzhu.com/datasketch/lsh.html) (formula), computed locally
- Nicholls (2019, International Journal of Communication) detects news text reuse at scale with w-shingles of 5-grams and 7-grams indexed in a database (163,297 UK articles). Prior work used 5-grams (Cagé et al. 2015), 7-grams (Citron & Ginsparg 2015), and 4-grams (Saridou et al. 2017). He excluded articles under 250 words and market-pricing items. Quoted text and PR-wire n-grams are flagged separately, and n-grams with huge frequency (some appear in more than 17,000 articles) are boilerplate. "75% of articles crediting the wires have at least 75% of wire content". For non-attributed content "75% of articles have 1% or less of wire content". Boumans et al. (2018) used a 0.65 cosine threshold — [Nicholls 2019 (IJoC)](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/9904)
- Kuntur et al. (arXiv, March 2026) trace editorial reuse from 15 foreign agencies (237,551 articles, 7 languages) into the Slovenian Press Agency (1,037 articles) with multilingual sentence embeddings at cosine 0.60. 52% of STA articles contained reuse. Only 4.4% of aligned pairs were 1:1 (54.9% many:many, 40.5% many:1), i.e., agency copy is paraphrased and recombined rather than copied verbatim — [arXiv 2603.29937](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29937v1)
- Dong, Berti-Equille & Srivastava (PVLDB 2009) introduce source dependence: sharing the same false values is evidence of copying. A Bayesian model with copy probability c and per-source error rate (the ACCU model, which also uses source accuracy) discounts copiers' votes during truth discovery. A companion paper handles dynamic copying ("Truth Discovery and Copying Detection in a Dynamic World", PVLDB 2009) — [PVLDB 2009](https://mail.vldb.org/pvldb/volumes/2/paper/Integrating%20Conflicting%20Data%3A%20The%20Role%20of%20Source%20Dependence); [Dong & Srivastava data fusion tutorial](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00310)
- URL canonicalization (w3lib/Scrapy canonicalize_url) sorts query arguments, normalizes percent-encoding to uppercase hex, drops default ports, and removes fragments by default. RFC 3986 normalization decodes percent-encoded unreserved characters — [w3lib canonicalize_url description](https://tessl.io/registry/tessl/pypi-w3lib/2.3.0/files/evals/scenario-2/task.md); [RFC 3986 section 6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3986#section-6)

### Inferences
Reproducible deterministic recipe. Every parameter should be versioned in a dedup_config_hash stored in each canonical observation.
1. Exact-key cascade, applied in order with first match wins:
   (a) source-native ID: GKGRECORDID, GlobalEventID, RSS guid when isPermaLink or stable, SerpApi result link plus query_id;
   (b) canonical URL key = SHA-256 of canon(url). Canonicalization: lowercase scheme and host; scheme forced to https; IDNA-encode host; strip leading "www.", "m.", "amp."; drop default ports; collapse duplicate slashes; strip trailing "/" and "/amp"; drop the fragment; drop query keys matching ^(utm_.*|fbclid|gclid|dclid|msclkid|mc_cid|mc_eid|igshid|ref|ref_src|cmpid|ocid|smid|guccounter)$; sort the remaining keys; uppercase percent-encoding. Freeze the tracker list in config. When a page is fetched, prefer the declared rel=canonical or og:url, but record both;
   (c) content hash = SHA-256 of normalized body text: Unicode NFKC, lowercase, collapse whitespace, strip boilerplate lines. The boilerplate list must be frozen and versioned.
2. Near-exact duplicates: 64-bit SimHash. Features are word 3-shingles of the normalized body with term-frequency weights. Feature hash is xxh64 with seed 0, or the first 8 bytes of BLAKE2b; avoid Python's built-in hash(), which is salted per process. Threshold is Hamming distance of 3 or less (Manku 2007). Use the 4-block, 16-bit pigeonhole index (k+1 = 4 blocks guarantees exact recall at k=3). By Charikar's formula, k=3 of 64 bits corresponds to an angle of about 3*pi/64, i.e., cosine about 0.989. So this catches only near-verbatim copies (reposts, AMP variants, trivial edits).
3. Edited syndication (wire rewrites, regional editions): MinHash with num_perm=128 and fixed seed=1 over 5-word shingles (Nicholls/Cagé). The LSH threshold is set below the decision threshold, for example LSH threshold 0.7, which gives (b=14, r=9) with P(0.8)=0.87. Then compute exact Jaccard on candidates and accept pairs with J of 0.8 or more. Also compute 7-gram containment C(A,B) = |S(A) ∩ S(B)| / |S(A)| and accept C of 0.5 or more as derivative-of. Exact verification keeps decisions deterministic given the shingle definition. LSH only affects recall, and with fixed seeds that recall is also reproducible. Insert documents in a fixed order (observed_time, then canonical URL key) so that tie-breaking for the "first seen" representative is deterministic.
4. Do not delete near-duplicates. Archive all observations and attach a copy_group_id (union-find over accepted pairs, with the root being the minimum (observed_time, url_key)). Dedup decisions then stay auditable and reversible, which Part 1's "deterministic dedup only" spec needs: only exact-key matches (step 1) collapse into one canonical observation, while steps 2-3 annotate.
5. Independence weighting for attention. Per narrative and window, count independent copy groups, not documents. Also collapse by registrable domain (eTLD+1) and by a curated owner map: the MEASURED iHeart and radio-network cases show that one corporate syndicate can account for hundreds of URLs. Dong et al.'s Bayesian copy detection is the principled next step, but it is stochastic and iterative. A deterministic simplification: weight w_g = 1 for each copy group plus alpha*(n_g - 1) for its extra members, with alpha fixed (for example 0.1) and disclosed. Then attention mass A = sum over groups of w_g. Report both raw document counts and independence-weighted counts to the Hawkes layer.
6. GKG-only documents (no body): use only steps 1(a)-(b) plus a metadata-signature flag (themes, persons, orgs, and tone identical, and metadata length above a threshold) as a syndication candidate, never as dedup. MEASURED false-merge cases (event listings, template pages) rule out treating it as identity.

### Gaps
- No published, reproducible benchmark of SimHash or MinHash thresholds specifically on modern news syndication (for example AP-to-local rewrites) with precision and recall. Nicholls reports distributions, not P/R at thresholds.
- No public, maintained owner or syndicate map of news domains was located in this pass (needed for owner-level collapse).
- No source found for the exact iHeart or Townsquare syndication structure; the MEASURED grouping is observational.

## 3. Topic Detection and Tracking, first story detection, streaming event detection, cross-document event coreference, entity linking, event coding schemes

### Takeaway
The TDT lineage maps cleanly onto the event-cluster level: single-pass nearest-neighbor clustering with a novelty threshold, made constant-time with random-hyperplane LSH (Petrović et al. 2010). It is deterministic if hyperplanes come from a fixed seed and documents arrive in a fixed order. The best documented production-style news stream clusterer (Miranda et al. 2018) is also single-pass, using TF-IDF plus entities plus timestamp features and a threshold. Neural cross-document event coreference remains far from solved (CoNLL F1 about 80 on ECB+ with gold mentions in 2024). Entity linking to Wikidata is feasible offline (ReFinED) but is a model-inference step that must be pinned by version.

### Cited Findings
- TDT (DARPA pilot, September 1996 to October 1997) defined segmentation, new event (first story) detection, and tracking from a few sample stories — [Allan et al. 1998 TDT Pilot Study Final Report](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~jgc/publication/Topic_Detection_Tracking_Pilot_DARPA_1998.pdf)
- Allan (ed., 2002) is the TDT book "Topic Detection and Tracking: Event-based Information Organization" — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4615-0933-2)
- Petrović, Osborne & Lavrenko (NAACL 2010). A new document is a first story if its nearest neighbor's cosine distance exceeds threshold t. LSH uses random hyperplanes with Pcoll = 1 - theta(x,y)/pi. The number of tables is L = log base (1 - Pcoll^k) of delta. They used k=13 bits and L chosen so that P(miss a neighbor within distance 0.2) is below 2.5%. TF-IDF uses incrementally updated IDF and the top 300 features per document. Variance reduction compares against the 2,000 most recent documents. Buckets are capped to keep time and space per document constant. The UMass baseline took 28 hours vs 2 hours for the LSH system, and the system was run over 163.5 million tweets — [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- MEASURED arithmetic: with cosine distance 0.2 (cos=0.8), Pcoll = 0.795, Pcoll^13 = 0.051, and L = ln(0.025)/ln(1-0.051) = 70.7, i.e., about 71 tables — derived from the formula in [Petrović et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021.pdf)
- Petrović, Osborne & Lavrenko (NAACL 2012) integrate paraphrases into LSH-based FSD and build a 50-million-tweet event corpus — [Petrović et al. 2012](https://aclanthology.org/N12-1034.pdf)
- Miranda et al. (EMNLP 2018), multilingual streaming news clustering (Priberam/LETA). Fully online. Monolingual clusters use TF-IDF subvectors over words, lemmas, and entities plus timestamp features (Gaussian on hour differences, sigma = 72 hours). A document joins the best cluster if similarity exceeds tau, otherwise it starts a new one. Crosslingual clusters link monolingual ones. Feature weights are learned with SVM-rank, beating CluStream. Datasets were 77k-134k documents per language. The method is robust to reordering within a 3-day window — [Miranda et al. 2018](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1483.pdf)
- USTORY (Yoon et al., SIGIR 2023) does unsupervised story discovery with theme- and time-aware dynamic embeddings over a frozen pretrained sentence encoder and novelty-aware adaptive clustering. SCStory (Yoon et al., WWW 2023) adds self-supervised continual learning of story-indicative embeddings with confidence-aware memory replay — [USTORY arXiv 2304.04099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04099); [SCStory arXiv 2312.03725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03725)
- Cross-document event coreference is evaluated with MUC, B3, CEAF-e, and CoNLL F1. Linear CDCR with X-AMR (2024) reaches CoNLL F1 79.8 on ECB+ dev. About 80 F1 is suggested as an upper bound for purely symbolic approaches. Older work reports about 69 CoNLL F1 on predicted settings (2018). A March 2026 paper systematically analyzes and unifies CDCR datasets — [X-AMR arXiv 2404.08656](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08656); [arXiv 1810.04216](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04216); [Cattan et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.01210); [arXiv 2603.00621](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00621)
- ReFinED (Amazon, NAACL Industry 2022) does mention detection, fine-grained typing, and disambiguation in one forward pass. It is more than 60 times faster than competing approaches, gains 3.7 F1 on average, and generalizes to Wikidata (15 times more entities than Wikipedia) zero-shot — [arXiv 2207.04108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04108); [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/publications/refined-an-efficient-zero-shot-capable-approach-to-end-to-end-entity-linking)
- CAMEO is the GDELT event taxonomy (three levels, root codes, four QuadClasses). PLOVER defines 16-18 event types aggregating the more than 250 CAMEO codes, with no 4-digit codes. POLECAT (Halterman et al. 2023), built on PLOVER, succeeds ICEWS — [Event Codebook V2.0](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf); [Halterman et al. 2023, arXiv 2304.01331](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.01331); [arXiv 2601.00430 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00430)
- EventKG holds more than 690k events and 2.3M temporal relations from Wikidata, DBpedia, YAGO, Wikipedia event lists, and the Current Events Portal, in 5 languages, using the Simple Event Model — [EventKG arXiv 1804.04526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.04526)

### Inferences
- Event-cluster level (L1 above observations). Use a Petrović/Miranda-style single-pass clusterer:
  - Representation: TF-IDF with IDF frozen from a pre-window corpus (or per-day IDF snapshots keyed by date), concatenated with entity IDs (GKG Persons and Organizations; Wikidata QIDs when linked).
  - Time: a Gaussian time kernel with sigma = 72h (Miranda).
  - Novelty: threshold tau tuned once on a labeled slice and then frozen.
  - Candidate retrieval: random-hyperplane LSH with k=13 and L of about 71. Generate hyperplanes from numpy Generator(Philox(key=fixed)) so they are identical across runs.
  - Order: process documents in (observed_time, url_key) order.
  This yields bit-reproducible event clusters for a given config hash. Dense sentence embeddings (USTORY-style) can replace TF-IDF if the encoder weights are pinned by hash and inference runs in deterministic mode on CPU.
- For the GDELT PoC, cluster on GKG fields only: Themes, Persons, Orgs, AllNames, locations, plus URL slug tokens. Full-text TF-IDF is possible only for documents fetched separately. URL slugs carry headline words (visible in the MEASURED URLs) and are a cheap deterministic text proxy.
- Do not rely on neural cross-document event coreference for identity. Use it only as optional edge evidence between event clusters, given the roughly 80 F1 ceiling. GDELT GlobalEventID linkages in Mentions can be one deterministic evidence source for merging event clusters.
- Entity linking: GKG names are strings, so add a deterministic alias table (Wikidata labels and aliases dump at a pinned date, then exact or normalized string match) as the baseline. Run ReFinED offline with a pinned model version only for fetched text. Record el_model_version in the envelope.
- Event coding: keep CAMEO as provided by GDELT. A CAMEO-to-PLOVER mapping can be a deterministic lookup table for a coarser event-type facet.

### Gaps
- No published FSD or story-detection evaluation on GDELT GKG specifically (fields only, no text) was found.
- ReFinED throughput on CPU (documents/s) was not reported in the sources fetched.
- No 2025-2026 benchmark found comparing LLM-based story clustering to USTORY or Miranda on news streams with reproducibility controls.

## 4. Narrative formation and evolution: dynamic topic models, streaming split/merge clustering, BERTopic dynamics, narrative extraction, event KGs, stable narrative identity

### Takeaway
No off-the-shelf method gives stable narrative identity in a stream by default. Dynamic topic models fix the number of topics. BERTopic's topics_over_time keeps global topics fixed and re-describes them per bin. DP-means and online CRP variants depend on arrival order. Narrative maps solve a small linear program over about 100 articles. The robust pattern is two-stage: (1) cluster per window deterministically, then (2) match clusters across windows with a fixed overlap rule (Jaccard or MONIC transitions: survive, split, merge, emerge, disappear). Narrative IDs are carried by lineage rules, never by re-clustering.

### Cited Findings
- Blei & Lafferty (ICML 2006) dynamic topic models chain the topics' natural parameters with a Gaussian state-space model. Inference uses variational Kalman filtering or wavelet regression because Gaussian and multinomial are non-conjugate. Demonstrated on Science 1880-2000. ICML Test of Time 2016. The number of topics K is fixed — [Blei & Lafferty 2006](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~blei/papers/BleiLafferty2006a.pdf); [Wikipedia DTM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_topic_model)
- Kulis & Jordan (ICML 2012) DP-means is the small-variance limit of a Gibbs sampler for a DP mixture: assign x_i to the nearest centroid unless min_c ||x_i - mu_c||^2 > lambda, in which case open a new cluster. It monotonically decreases the objective sum ||x - mu||^2 + lambda*k. "unlike standard k-means, which depends on the initial clustering of the data, the DP-means algorithm depends on the order in which data points are processed" — [Kulis & Jordan arXiv 1111.0352](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.0352)
- BERTopic topics_over_time fits global topics first, then computes c-TF-IDF per topic and timestamp, with global_tuning (average with the global representation) and evolution_tuning (average with the previous timestep). "the topic itself remains the same" while its representation changes. nr_bins controls bins — [BERTopic DTM docs](https://maartengr.github.io/BERTopic/getting_started/topicsovertime/topicsovertime.html)
- BERTopic online mode supports partial_fit with IncrementalPCA, MiniBatchKMeans (random_state set in examples), River DBSTREAM (which creates new clusters), and OnlineCountVectorizer with decay and delete_min_df. merge_models is offered as an alternative for adding new topics. Only the most recent batch is tracked in .topics_ — [BERTopic online docs](https://maartengr.github.io/BERTopic/getting_started/online/online.html)
- Narrative maps (Keith Norambuena & Mitra, PACM HCI CSCW 2020; often cited as 2021) represent narratives as route maps of events. Coherence comes from Universal Sentence Encoder v4 headline embeddings (angular similarity) plus UMAP+HDBSCAN topic clusters. A linear program maximizes coherence under coverage and structure constraints. The case study used 102 articles and produced an optimal map of 22 events — [Narrative Maps CSCW 2020](https://faculty.washington.edu/tmitra/public/papers/cscw2020-narrativeMaps.pdf)
- A follow-up user study (2021) of 10 analysts building maps from 40 articles found 7 connection types (temporal, similarity, entity, topical, causal, speculative, domain knowledge) and structures ranging from lists to DAGs — [arXiv 2108.06035](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2108.06035)
- RELATIO (Ash, Gauthier & Widmer, Political Analysis, online 2023, vol. 32 2024) extracts agent-verb-patient narrative statements via semantic role labeling and clusters entities into coherent groups. Applied to the US Congressional Record — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/E72C0482A44C9A817E381B394A73E2D6); [Bocconi PDF](https://iris.unibocconi.it/retrieve/1e452d9c-8223-4007-bdad-b54c116fe72f/relatio-text-semantics-capture-political-and-economic-narratives.pdf)
- MONIC (Spiliopoulou et al., KDD 2006) defines external cluster transitions (survived, merged, split, disappeared, emerged) and internal ones (location and cohesion change) between clusterings at successive times — [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/SpiliopoulouNTS06/bibtex); [R clusTransition](https://cran.uni-muenster.de/web/packages/clusTransition/refman/clusTransition.html)
- Greene, Doyle & Cunningham (2010) detect communities per snapshot, then match them across snapshots by Jaccard above a user threshold, allowing many-to-many mappings and evolutionary events (birth, death, merge, split, continuation) — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/tracking-the-evolution-of-communities-in-dynamic-social-networks/); [code](https://github.com/derekgreene/dynamic-community/blob/master/README.md)
- EventKG gives Wikidata-anchored event nodes and temporal relations usable as external anchors — [EventKG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.04526)

### Inferences
Recommended narrative layer, deterministic-first:
- (a) Per window W (for example 6h or 24h), cluster event clusters (from Key Question 3) into narrative candidates with a deterministic algorithm. Options: DP-means with fixed lambda and fixed processing order (observed_time, id), and the global mean as initialization as in Kulis & Jordan; or agglomerative clustering with a fixed linkage and distance cut (deterministic up to tie-breaking, so break ties by ID).
- (b) Match candidates in W to narratives alive in W-1 using the observation-overlap Jaccard J(C, N) = |C ∩ N| / |C ∪ N| over observation IDs. Fall back to entity-set Jaccard when overlap is small. Use a frozen threshold (Greene used a user threshold; 0.3 is a common choice but must be tuned and disclosed). Apply MONIC-style rules:
  - one-to-one above threshold: survive (keep the ID);
  - one old to many new: split (the largest child keeps the ID, others get new IDs with parent pointers);
  - many old to one new: merge (the oldest ID survives, others are marked merged_into);
  - no match: emerge;
  - unmatched old narrative: dormant, then disappeared after M windows.
  Persist every transition as an immutable lineage edge so the Hawkes layer can re-aggregate attention along lineages.
- (c) Use BERTopic topics_over_time only for descriptive labels of fixed narratives. Its UMAP stage is stochastic. If used, set random_state and accept that UMAP is not guaranteed bit-identical across hardware or thread counts. Prefer IncrementalPCA, which is deterministic per the docs, or skip dimension reduction.
- (d) Use narrative maps (LP) and RELATIO (SRL triples) offline, per narrative, to explain a narrative's internal structure for forensics. They are not scalable as streaming identity mechanisms (the LP case study had 102 articles).
- (e) Anchor theme-level nodes to stable external IDs where possible: GKG theme codes, Wikidata QIDs, EventKG events. Theme identity is then exogenous and stable by construction.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures narrative-ID stability (churn, purity over time) for news streams under split and merge matching. The thresholds above need empirical tuning on the PoC window.
- No reference implementation found of an online CRP or DP-means variant with explicit, documented deterministic ordering for news.

## 5. Soft membership: assigning documents to multiple narratives, calibrating weights, propagating uncertainty to counts

### Takeaway
Make membership a deterministic function of frozen scores: a softmax over similarity to narrative centroids with a fixed temperature, plus an explicit "none" option, truncated to the top m. Calibrate the temperature once on a labeled slice. Propagate uncertainty analytically: expected attention = sum of membership probabilities, with Poisson-binomial variance = sum of p(1-p). Optionally draw reproducible multiple imputations with a counter-based RNG keyed by (observation_id, narrative_id, draw).

### Cited Findings
- HDBSCAN soft clustering: all_points_membership_vectors returns membership_vectors[i, j] = probability that point i belongs to cluster j. It requires fitting with prediction_data=True. membership_vector() handles new points — [hdbscan docs](https://hdbscan.readthedocs.io/en/latest/soft_clustering.html); [cuML docs](https://docs.rapids.ai/api/cuml/stable/api/generated/cuml.cluster.hdbscan.all_points_membership_vectors/)
- Mixed-membership topic models (LDA family, DTM) give each document a distribution over topics. DTM's per-document topic proportions come from variational inference (stochastic initialization) — [Blei & Lafferty 2006](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~blei/papers/BleiLafferty2006a.pdf)
- Temperature scaling (a single scalar T on logits, fitted on held-out data) is a simple, effective post-hoc calibration method for neural classifiers (Guo et al. 2017) — [arXiv 1706.04599](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)
- For a sum of independent Bernoulli(p_i), the distribution is Poisson binomial with mean sum p_i and variance sum p_i(1 - p_i) — [Wikipedia: Poisson binomial distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_binomial_distribution)
- numpy's Philox is a counter-based bit generator: given a key and counter it yields a reproducible stream, suitable for keyed, order-independent random draws — [numpy Philox docs](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/bit_generators/philox.html)
- MEASURED attention scale on 2025-01-27 (English GKG): documents matching "deepseek" in AllNames, Orgs, or URL numbered 1,417 from 681 distinct sources, peaking at 47 per 15-min batch. "nvidia": 1,863 documents from 790 sources, peaking at 55 per batch. Documents with the GKG theme ECON_STOCKMARKET: 6,183 — [GDELT raw files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/20250127120000.gkg.csv.zip)

### Inferences
- Membership function (deterministic):
  - Score: s_in = cos(x_i, mu_n) for the top candidate narratives from the ANN or LSH index, plus a null narrative with fixed score s_0.
  - Probability: p_in = exp(s_in / T) / (exp(s_0 / T) + sum over j of exp(s_ij / T)), keeping the top m = 3 and renormalizing.
  - Fit T and s_0 once on 200-500 hand-labeled (observation, narrative) pairs from the PoC window by minimizing negative log-likelihood, then freeze them.
  - Record membership_model_version and the centroid snapshot ID used for each window. Centroids are frozen at window start, so membership never depends on intra-window arrival order.
- Attention mass per narrative n, window w: E[A_nw] = sum over i in w of p_in * v_i, where v_i is the independence weight from Key Question 2. Var[A_nw] = sum over i of p_in (1 - p_in) v_i^2, assuming independent memberships. Positive dependence (copy groups assigned together) makes this an underestimate, so either apply it at the copy-group level (one membership per group) or report it as a lower bound.
- Hawkes input options: (1) feed E[A] as the mark or count and carry Var[A] as measurement-error variance; (2) multiple imputation with K = 20-50 draws. Draw d for observation i uses u = Philox(key = SHA-256(obs_id || narrative_snapshot_id)[:16], counter = d), and observation i is assigned to narrative n if u falls in n's cumulative-probability interval. Draws are then reproducible and independent of processing order. The Hawkes model is fit per draw and the parameter estimates pooled.
- The MEASURED counts show the scale. Even the biggest story of the window gives tens of documents per 15-min bin in English GKG, so Poisson noise of about sqrt(47), or about 7, per bin is comparable to the membership uncertainty. Report both.
- HDBSCAN membership vectors are an alternative, but they depend on the full fit (density-based, recomputed per refit). They suit offline windows rather than a frozen online assignment function.

### Gaps
- No news-specific study found on calibrating soft narrative membership or on how membership uncertainty biases Hawkes branching-ratio estimates. This needs simulation in the PoC.

## 6. Hierarchical summarization and level of detail (observation, event cluster, narrative, theme), graph coarsening, hot/warm/cold tiers

### Takeaway
Build the levels of detail bottom-up with explicit, deterministic aggregation edges (observation to event cluster to narrative to theme) rather than a stochastic hierarchical topic model. Use graph coarsening theory (Loukas 2019) as the justification for summarizing a node set by its aggregate. Tier storage by time: hot (in memory, active windows), warm (columnar files, recent weeks), cold (immutable, content-addressed archive). The tiers reflect level-of-detail retention, not separate truths.

### Cited Findings
- Hierarchical LDA and the nested Chinese restaurant process (Blei, Griffiths & Jordan, JACM 2010; NIPS 2003) model documents as paths down an infinitely deep and wide topic tree learned by Bayesian nonparametric inference (MCMC, so stochastic) — [arXiv 0710.0845](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.0845); [NeurIPS 2003](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2003/hash/7b41bfa5085806dfa24b8c9de0ce567f-Abstract.html)
- Loukas (JMLR 2019) gives multilevel graph coarsening with restricted spectral approximation guarantees: sufficient conditions for a coarse graph to preserve spectral and cut properties of the original, with code available — [JMLR v20 18-680](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v20/18-680.html); [code](https://github.com/loukasa/graph-coarsening)
- GKG themes are a controlled vocabulary (GDELT themes plus the World Bank Topical Ontology, among others) usable as an exogenous top layer — [SUERF/Amundi](https://www.suerf.org/publications/suerf-policy-notes-and-briefs/monitoring-narratives-an-application-to-the-equity-market/); [GKG Codebook V2.1](http://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Global_Knowledge_Graph_Codebook-V2.1.pdf)
- MEASURED storage: one day of English GKG reduced to 8 columns (ID, DATE, source, URL, Themes, Persons, Orgs, Tone) is 16.9 MB as Parquet, vs 533.9 MB zipped raw — [GDELT raw files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/20250127120000.gkg.csv.zip)

### Inferences
- Level-of-detail graph:
  - L0 observation: the canonical observation.
  - L1 event cluster: single-pass clustering from Key Question 3.
  - L2 narrative: windowed clustering plus lineage matching from Key Question 4.
  - L3 theme: an exogenous taxonomy (GKG themes or WB ontology, plus curated market themes) or deterministic agglomeration of narratives by entity and theme overlap.
  - Each parent stores aggregates over its children: counts, independence-weighted mass, E and Var of attention, top entities, centroid, first-seen and last-seen times. Coarsening is therefore lossless for the attention statistics the Hawkes layer needs, sums being additive.
- Do not use hLDA or nCRP for structural identity: it is MCMC-based and re-inference changes the tree. Use it at most offline to suggest theme groupings.
- Tiering:
  - Hot: the last 24-72h of observations, open event clusters, narratives touched in the last 7 days, LSH/ANN indices, and centroids, held in memory.
  - Warm: 7-90 days in Parquet/DuckDB partitioned by observed date, holding full L0 rows plus L1-L3 aggregates.
  - Cold: the immutable raw archive (original GDELT zips verified against manifest md5 and stored by SHA-256, plus RSS/SerpApi raw payloads) and per-window aggregate snapshots.
  - Demotion is by time and inactivity. A dormant narrative keeps its centroid and lineage in warm storage so it can be reactivated (a MONIC "survive" after a gap) without re-reading cold storage.
  - MEASURED compression implies a 3-week window of 8-column GKG fits in about 0.36 GB of Parquet.

### Gaps
- No published system found that applies spectral graph coarsening specifically to news event or narrative graphs. The link is by analogy.
- No benchmark found for hot-tier sizes in comparable news systems.

## 7. Streaming infrastructure facts for determinism: exactly-once and idempotence (NATS JetStream, Kafka), deterministic replay, content-addressed storage

### Takeaway
Neither NATS nor Kafka gives end-to-end exactly-once into external state. Both give deduplicated publish plus at-least-once delivery, so correctness must come from idempotent consumers keyed by the observation's deterministic ID. Make every message ID a content-derived hash. Replay comes from the immutable archive in a fixed order, not from broker retention.

### Cited Findings
- NATS JetStream: a stream's Duplicate Window defaults to 2m0s. "For two minutes after a message is stored, the server turns away a second message that carries the same Nats-Msg-Id header" — [NATS docs: Streams](https://docs.nats.io/nats-concepts/jetstream/streams)
- JetStream supports exactly-once by combining message deduplication (Nats-Msg-Id, checked on the ID only, not the body) with double acks. A reply subject on an ack makes the server acknowledge the ack. Consumers wanting exactly-once processing must guard against acknowledging another consumer's redelivery — [NATS docs: Model deep dive](https://docs.nats.io/using-nats/jetstream/model_deep_dive)
- Kafka KIP-98 introduced idempotent producers (producer ID plus per-partition sequence numbers, duplicates discarded by the broker; enable.idempotence=true), transactions for atomic multi-partition writes and offset commits (transactional.id), and isolation.level=read_committed consumers — [KIP-98](https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/KAFKA/KIP-98+-+Exactly+Once+Delivery+and+Transactional+Messaging)
- Git stores objects under the hash of their content (header plus content), giving immutable, deduplicated, content-addressed storage — [Git Internals: Git Objects](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Git-Internals-Git-Objects)
- IPFS content identifiers (CIDs) are derived from a multihash of the content, so identical content gets the same address — [IPFS docs: content addressing](https://docs.ipfs.tech/concepts/content-addressing/)
- GDELT manifests provide size and md5 per file, which can verify archive integrity on ingest — [GDELT lastupdate.txt](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/lastupdate.txt)
- GDELT's DOC 2.0 API returned HTTP 429 on 2026-10-03 with "Please limit requests to one every 5 seconds", pointing heavy users to the web ngrams dataset. This is relevant if the API serves as an on-demand sensor alongside SerpApi — [GDELT DOC API response](https://api.gdeltproject.org/api/v2/doc/doc); [GDELT ngrams blog](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/using-the-new-web-ngrams-dataset-to-find-relevant-coverage/)

### Inferences
- IDs:
  - raw_blob_id = SHA-256 of the raw bytes (GDELT zip, RSS XML, SerpApi JSON).
  - observation_id = SHA-256(source_type || source_native_id || canonical_url_key || content_hash_or_empty), so it is deterministic and stable across replays.
  - Use observation_id as Nats-Msg-Id, or as the Kafka record key.
  - Consumers upsert by observation_id. Use a SQLite or DuckDB table with a PRIMARY KEY, or append-only Parquet plus a dedup pass. Duplicates then have no effect, whatever the broker window.
- The NATS 2-minute default dedup window is too short to protect against replays or GDELT re-downloads hours later. Set the window explicitly (for example 24h, and disclose it) or rely on consumer idempotence. Prefer the latter.
- Deterministic replay contract:
  - Input: the ordered list of archive blobs (GDELT batch timestamp order, then file type order export, mentions, gkg, translation.*), plus RSS and SerpApi payloads ordered by their recorded observed_time.
  - Config: one config hash covering canonicalization rules, tracker list, shingle sizes, seeds, thresholds, model versions, IDF snapshot, and centroid snapshot.
  - Output: derived tables are a pure function of (inputs, config hash). Verify with a run manifest that records SHA-256 of every output partition.
- For a one-machine PoC, a broker is optional. A directory-based log works: one file per batch, named by timestamp and hash. Run NATS JetStream with file storage only if the live path is being exercised. Kafka is heavier than necessary on 4 cores and 15 GB.
- Floating-point determinism: polars, numpy, and BLAS reductions can vary with thread count. Pin thread counts (POLARS_MAX_THREADS, OMP_NUM_THREADS) for replays that must be bit-identical, or round similarity scores (for example to 1e-6) before threshold comparisons.

### Gaps
- Official Kafka documentation pages could not be fetched in text form (JS-rendered). Kafka facts rely on KIP-98.
- The exact JetStream server-side maximum for the duplicate window was not confirmed in fetched docs.

## 8. Feasibility of a Python PoC on one machine (4 cores, 15 GB RAM): throughput for parsing and deduplicating GKG for a 1-3 week replay, and what to disclose

### Takeaway
Feasible with a large margin. MEASURED on the target-class machine, one full day of English GKG (533.9 MB zipped, 1.65 GB unzipped, 130,852 records) unzips and parses with polars in 11.5 s (about 144 MB/s, about 11,400 records/s). The full Events plus Mentions day takes about 2 s, peak RSS is about 2.1 GB, and the whole one-day benchmark including canonicalization and dedup statistics ran in 21 s wall time. A 3-week English replay therefore parses in about 4-5 minutes. Adding Translingual (about 1.7 times more bytes) remains well under 15 minutes. The bottlenecks are pure-Python per-document work (canonicalization about 62k URLs/s, SimHash about 27k docs/s with numpy, MinHash about 9k docs/s) and any embedding or entity-linking step. Network download time (about 1.8 MB/s observed here: 555 MB for the full English day in about 5 minutes) is the slowest step for a cold start.

### Cited Findings
**Method (MEASURED, 2026-10-03)**
- Downloaded all 288 English files for 2025-01-27 (96 each of gkg, export, and mentions). All returned HTTP 200. Read with polars.read_csv (tab separator, no quoting, all columns as strings, utf8-lossy) after in-memory unzip; one process; polars multi-threaded on 4 cores — [GDELT raw files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/20250127120000.gkg.csv.zip)

**Results**
- GKG English day: parse plus column select took 11.5 s (144 MB/s unzipped, 11,410 records/s). Peak RSS was about 0.9 GB after loading the day (12 columns kept), and about 2.1 GB for the entire script, which also loaded the Events and Mentions days. Events day (130,388 rows × 61 cols) parsed in 1.2 s and Mentions day (378,225 rows) in 0.9 s — [GDELT raw files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/20250127120000.gkg.csv.zip)
- Day sizes on 2025-01-27: GKG English 533.9 MB zipped (inside the user's measured 520-630 MB/day range), Events 8.4 MB, Mentions 12.4 MB (Events plus Mentions about 20.8 MB, matching the user's about 20 MB/day), GKG Translingual 908.6 MB zipped — [GDELT raw files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/20250127120000.translation.gkg.csv.zip)
- Dedup and annotation throughput (pure Python unless noted): URL canonicalization about 61,600/s; MinHash.bulk (128 perms) about 9,300 docs/s; LSH query plus insert about 14k-15k docs/s; SimHash about 3,600 docs/s pure Python or about 26,900 docs/s with numpy; exact Hamming search for k of 3 or less over 20k fingerprints via the 4-block index in 0.4 s — computed locally on [GDELT raw files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/20250127120000.gkg.csv.zip)
- GDELT DOC API rate limit: one request per 5 seconds (HTTP 429 message, 2026-10-03) — [GDELT DOC API](https://api.gdeltproject.org/api/v2/doc/doc)

### Inferences
- Budget for a 3-week window (21 days), English only:
  - Download: about 11.2-13.2 GB of GKG zips (21 × 534-630 MB) plus about 0.44 GB Events and Mentions. At the about 1.8 MB/s observed here, that is roughly 2 hours, so download once into the content-addressed archive.
  - Parse: about 21 × 11.5 s, i.e., about 4 min.
  - URL canonicalization: about 2.75M documents at 62k/s, i.e., about 45 s.
  - Columnar store: about 0.36 GB of 8-column Parquet.
  With Translingual: add about 19 GB of zips and an estimated 2.8 times more documents. Parsing stays at about 10-15 min. The extrapolation from one 12:00 batch ratio is uncertain.
- Memory plan: process batch by batch (about 17 MB unzipped per English batch) and stream reduced columns to Parquet. Never hold GCAM for more than one batch. Keep only V2Themes or Themes, Persons, Orgs, AllNames, Tone, URL, and source in the hot tier. A 21-day hot set of reduced columns fits comfortably in under 4 GB.
- Text-level near-dup (SimHash and MinHash on bodies) is only needed for fetched documents (RSS and SerpApi), which are orders of magnitude fewer than GKG rows. Pure Python is sufficient.
- Embedding all GKG documents is the main potential cost. Avoid it in the PoC: use GKG fields plus URL-slug TF-IDF for clustering, and reserve embeddings for fetched text and for narrative-level centroids.
- Filtering and sampling choices to disclose:
  - English-only vs plus Translingual: English-only drops about 74% of documents by count in the measured batch and shifts geography.
  - GDELT source universe: about 7.8k domains per day English. GDELT's selection is opaque and drifts; one live 2026 batch was less than half the January 2025 per-batch size.
  - URL canonicalization rule set and tracker list version.
  - Whether near-duplicates are collapsed or only downweighted, and the alpha in the independence weights.
  - Mention Confidence or InRawText filters for any event-level series.
  - The lag constant used to simulate ingested_time in replay.
  - Use of GKG DATE (batch time) as a proxy for publication time.
  - Any keyword or theme pre-filters (for example keeping ECON_* and tech themes or a seed entity list), with recall implications: the 2025-01-27 "deepseek" match set was only 1,417 English GKG documents.
  - Missing batches, if any, in the window. All 96 were present for 2025-01-27; other days were not checked.
  - Thread-count pinning for bit-identical replays.
- Physics sanity check on the attention signal: at peaks of about 50 documents per 15 min for the top story, a counting process sampled at 15-min resolution sits in the low-count regime where Poisson shot noise (relative error about 1/sqrt(N), about 14%) is not negligible. That argues for a continuous-time Hawkes fit on timestamps rather than on binned counts, with independence-weighted marks.

### Gaps
- Only one day (2025-01-27) was benchmarked. Volume on 2025-01-20 to 01-26 was not measured, so the DeepSeek attention ramp before the NVIDIA drop is not yet quantified. Downloading the remaining days takes about 5 minutes per day of English GKG at the observed bandwidth.
- Translingual document counts per day are extrapolated from one batch.
- Embedding and entity-linking throughput on this CPU-only machine was not measured.
- Not measured: RSS feed volumes and SerpApi query budgets (out of scope of the GDELT measurements).
