Yggdrasil Research Brief

Yggdrasil watches the world's news all the time, builds a graph of narratives and the attention each one gets, and, when a stock moves abnormally, searches that graph for competing explanations and proves which ones the evidence supports. This brief condenses six research tracks and a 25,600-word report into what changed, the architecture, and the mathematics, with animations where motion explains better than text.

One correction the film makes

clingo does not generate the competing explanations. The search does: it returns the cheapest explanation through each entry point, plus "we do not know". clingo then judges that fixed list. Letting clingo search an open space of explanations would be logic-based abduction, which is Σ2P-complete; judging a fixed list keeps every verdict inside NP and coNP.

1 · IngestionSpecified
\[ \mathtt{raw\_blob\_id} = \mathrm{sha256}(\mathtt{raw\_bytes}) \]
\[ \mathrm{window}(o) = \left\lfloor \frac{t_{\mathrm{obs}}(o) - t_0}{\Delta} \right\rfloor \]
  • sha256: a content address. The same bytes always get the same id, so replays and re-downloads change nothing.
  • Δ = 15 minutes, GDELT's batch heartbeat; t₀ is the start of the replay.
  • Measured: 96 batches and 130,852 English documents on 27 January 2025.

A seismograph drum: the world is sampled at a fixed rate, and nothing recorded is ever overwritten.

2 · DeduplicationSpecified
\[ v_o = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{first copy in its group} \\ \alpha_{\mathrm{copy}} = 0.1 & \text{later copies} \end{cases} \]
\[ \text{group mass} = 1 + \alpha_{\mathrm{copy}}\,(n-1) \]
  • Exact keys collapse records: native id, then canonical URL, then content hash. First match wins.
  • Near-copies (SimHash, MinHash) are never deleted; they only lower the weight of later copies.
  • n is the number of copies; one story on 271 iHeart stations counts 28 units, not 271.

Count witnesses, not echoes. A rumor repeated by 271 radio stations is still one source.

3 · Objects and clocksSpecified
\[ \mathrm{admissible}(o, \tau^{*}) \;=\; \mathtt{first\_seen}_o < \tau^{*} \]
  • Five clocks per object: event, published, observed, ingested, first seen.
  • first_seen is when Yggdrasil first held the object; τ* is the moment the market first showed the move.
  • A story first seen after τ* (the distillation allegations, 29 January) can never explain the move.

Chain of custody: evidence counts from the moment we first held it, not from the date printed on it.

4 · Cold-starting narrativesPartly open
\[ p_{o,n} = \frac{e^{s_{o,n}/T}}{Z_o} \]
\[ y_{n,t} = \sum_{o} v_o\, p_{o,n} \]
\[ \sum_{n\ge1} y_{n,t} + y_{0,t} = V_t \]
  • s is the similarity of article o to narrative n's centroid; T is a calibrated temperature; n = 0 is the null narrative "none".
  • y is attention mass: a weighted soft count. Each article's single unit is split, never copied, so the total equals ingested volume V.
  • Cold start: three weeks of history (three times the longest memory) set the priors, and traces start at their historical means.
  • A split hands memory to the children in proportion to overlap, z_c = q_c z_N, so nothing is created or lost.

A prism: one beam of light, one article, is split across colors without adding any light.

Open: the seed narratives, membership calibration (200 to 500 labeled pairs), the lineage threshold, and the rule for brand-new narratives (a conjecture). The hackathon seeds from GDELT themes.

5 · How objects move attentionSpecified, not fitted
\[ z_{j,m,t} = a_m\, z_{j,m,t-1} + (1-a_m)\, y_{j,t-1} \]
\[ \eta_i = \mathrm{base}_i + \sum_{j,m} A_{ij,m}\, z_{j,m} \]
\[ \lambda_i = s\log\!\left(1+e^{\eta_i/s}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{B}{B+S^{\mathrm{raw}}}\right)^{\omega} \]
  • a_m = exp(−Δ/τ_m) is how much memory survives one window, at timescales 1 h, 6 h, 1 day, 1 week; 1 − a_m is the share of new mass that enters.
  • base: outside inflow (hour of week, CPI, FOMC, earnings). A_ij,m: attention units on i caused per unit on j at timescale m.
  • softplus keeps the rate positive and is linear once large. The last factor is one gain shared by every narrative: ω = 0 means no competition, ω = 1 a hard capacity B.
  • Fitted by a convex sparse group lasso under a quasi-Poisson likelihood; a zero group means no edge.

Each trace is an RC circuit, a capacitor charged by news and leaking with time constant τ. Competition is automatic gain control in a radio: when the band gets loud, every station is turned down by the same factor.

Not fitted yet: needs the 6 to 27 January 2025 GDELT replay. ω, the timescales and every edge are hypotheses that experiments F1 to F4 decide.

6 · New information and transition weightsSpecified
\[ r_{i,t} = \frac{y_{i,t}-\lambda_{i,t}}{\sqrt{\varphi\,\lambda_{i,t}}} \]
\[ \alpha_{j\to i} = \frac{\sigma(\eta_i/s)\sum_m A_{ij,m}\, z_{j,m}}{\lambda^{\mathrm{raw}}_i} \]
\[ p(\mathrm{BOT}\to i) = 1-\sum_j \alpha_{j\to i} \]
  • r is the surprise: what arrived minus what the graph predicted, in units of its own noise; φ is overdispersion.
  • R1's release: (400 − 43.5) / √43.5 ≈ 54σ, so new information entered the AI narrative through BOT.
  • α is the share of i's attention that j caused this window. The shares, plus BOT's remainder, are the edge probabilities the search uses (toy values 0.20 / 0.44 / 0.36).

Gauss's law on a graph: attention the flows cannot account for must have a source, and that source is the outside world.

7 · An anomaly is observedPartly open
\[ AR_t = R_t - \left(\alpha + \beta R^{m}_t + \gamma R^{\mathrm{sec}}_t\right) \]
\[ M_z = 0.6745\cdot\frac{AR - \mathrm{median}}{\mathrm{MAD}} \]
  • AR is the abnormal return: the move minus what the market and sector would have predicted.
  • M_z is a robust z-score: medians and MAD, so one crash cannot inflate its own yardstick.
  • A case opens if |SAR| ≥ 4 or |M_z| ≥ 5 and a jump test or abnormal volume agrees. Same-day movers (NVDA −16.9%, AVGO −17.4%, VST −28.3%, CEG −20.9%) form one cluster case.
  • τ* is the earliest first abnormal print in the cluster, which may be pre-market or the European open.

A seismometer trigger: only a jolt far beyond the background noise opens an investigation.

Open: the price data source (no intraday prices obtained yet), the thresholds, and the overnight-gap cutoff rule. Bars before 27 January in the film are illustrative.

8 · Searching for the smallest explanationSpecified
\[ c(u\to v) = -\log p(u\to v) + \lambda_{\mathrm{node}} \]
\[ \Gamma = \min_x \min\!\left(-\log\frac{R_x}{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{L}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{node}},\; (L+1)\,\lambda_{\mathrm{node}}\right) \]
  • −log p is the surprise of an edge in nats; λ_node is a price per extra node, which is what makes the answer parsimonious.
  • Push sends probability mass backwards from the market events; R_x is the mass left unpushed.
  • Exact dynamic programming over (node, set of events): grow a tree upward, or merge two trees at the same node. The first time (BOT, all events) is popped, its cost is optimal.
  • If the best cost is at most Γ, nothing the push never reached can beat it: the answer is the smallest in the whole graph.

Cost is energy. The best explanation is the ground state, rivals are excited states, and their odds are Boltzmann factors exp(−ΔE).

Open: λ_node, the push settings (α, ε, L), and the abstention prior p₀. The film uses toy numbers.

9 · Competing explanationsSpecified
\[ \mathrm{odds}(S_1 : S_2) = e^{\,\mathrm{cost}(S_2)-\mathrm{cost}(S_1)} \]
  • The best explanation through each entry point (Lemma 11.2), plus "we do not know", reported if within log 20 of the best.
  • Toy example: R1 release 5.39 (best), capex 5.79 (odds 1.5 : 1), we do not know 6.59 (odds 3.3 : 1).
  • The search generates the competing explanations. clingo never invents them; it judges them. Open-ended abduction would be Σ2P-complete.

Like ranking routes on a map: the best route through each starting junction, compared by total travel cost.

10 · From subgraph to clingo verdictsPartly designed
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{SUPPORTED} &\iff c_{\mathrm{IN}} \\ \mathrm{CONTRADICTED} &\iff c_{\mathrm{OUT}} \\ \mathrm{UNRESOLVED} &\iff b_{\mathrm{OUT}} \wedge \neg c_{\mathrm{OUT}} \\ \mathrm{CONSISTENT\text{-}BUT\text{-}UNPROVEN} &\iff \neg b_{\mathrm{OUT}} \wedge \neg c_{\mathrm{IN}} \end{aligned} \]
  • Graph-derived facts are deterministic: trigger(h, event), before(event, move), signature_ok(h), schema_refuter(h, claim).
  • Each report is assumed accurate (ok) unless an independent contrary report of equal or better tier overrides it; claims hold only through accepted reports.
  • Each consistent reading is a stable model. b = in some reading, c = in every reading, for expl(H) (IN) and refuted(H) (OUT).
  • Toy verdicts that match the report's predictions: H1 consistent but unproven, H3 ($5.6M all-in) contradicted, H4 (chip smuggling) unresolved, H6 (attention cascade) supported.

Two witnesses of equal standing contradict each other: the court keeps both readings open and calls the matter unresolved.

Not designed yet: turning article text into reported(...) facts (claim extraction), explanation schemas for each hypothesis type, and the source-tier policy.

  • clingo and CaDiCaL are untrusted; their witness models and LRAT refutation proofs are checked by a Lean 4 checker proved sound once.

A notary checks the paperwork, not the facts: the certificate guarantees the reasoning, not the evidence.

Biggest structural finding · Lemma 5.1, derived

Yggdrasil's own measurement already conserves attention. Each article's soft memberships sum to one, including a "none of the above" narrative, so in every window the tracked attention plus the null narrative equals ingested volume exactly:

\[ \sum_{n=1}^{N} y_{n,t} + y_{0,t} = V_t \]

So "is attention conserved?" is trivially yes and tells you nothing. The real question is how strongly narratives compete for the supply the newswires are expected to deliver, and the exponent ω measures exactly that.

One deterministic function from news archive to proven verdict

Theorem 3.1, derived · end-to-end replay determinism

If no stage reads the wall clock, every random draw comes from a counter-based generator keyed on the configuration and record IDs, every floating-point reduction runs in a fixed order on a pinned binary, and every tie is broken by a fixed key, then every table and every verdict is a pure function of the archived input and the frozen configuration. Proof by induction over the ordered input stream.

Attention model

Every event raises the rate, and the raise fades

The exact model, one 15-minute window at a time

Narrative i's attention mass in window t is \(y_{i,t}\). Four memory traces per narrative remember it at different timescales, the drive combines baseline and excitation, softplus keeps the rate positive, and a common factor applies competition:

\[ z_{j,m,t} = a_m\, z_{j,m,t-1} + (1-a_m)\, y_{j,t-1}, \qquad a_m = e^{-\Delta/\tau_m}, \quad \tau \in \{1\text{h}, 6\text{h}, 1\text{d}, 1\text{w}\} \]
\[ \eta_{i,t} = \text{base}_{i,t} + \sum_{j}\sum_{m} A_{ij,m}\, z_{j,m,t}, \qquad \lambda^{\text{raw}}_{i,t} = s\,\log\!\left(1 + e^{\eta_{i,t}/s}\right) \]
\[ \lambda_{i,t} = \lambda^{\text{raw}}_{i,t}\left(\frac{B_t}{B_t + S^{\text{raw}}_t}\right)^{\omega}, \qquad S^{\text{raw}}_t = \sum_i \lambda^{\text{raw}}_{i,t}, \qquad y_{i,t} \sim \text{quasi-Poisson}(\lambda_{i,t}) \]

Lemma 6.5, derived · bounded attention

With ω = 0, mean attention stays bounded if the spectral radius of the positive parts of A, times the maximum hour-of-week gain, is below 1. Inhibitory weights never hurt. With any ω > 0, mean attention is bounded for any excitation strength at all, and with ω = 1 the total is below B on every path.

Lemma 6.6, derived · the 15-minute grid

For a 1-hour kernel, on average 11.5% of a parent's offspring land in the parent's own bin, where a model with lags ≥ 1 cannot see them. The fitted coefficient is then low by an exact factor, 0.885 for the 1-hour kernel, and the correction is:

\[ \hat A_{\text{cont}} = \hat A_{\text{disc}} \cdot \frac{x}{1-e^{-x}}, \qquad x = \frac{\Delta}{\tau} \]
Semi-conservation

Narratives compete by division, and ω says how much

Proposition 6.2 (divisive, derived). Ratios are preserved, the total is capped by \(B^{\omega} (S^{\text{raw}})^{1-\omega}\), more drive never lowers the total, and splitting or merging a narrative leaves every other narrative unchanged. At ω = 1 it is exactly the steady state of Grossberg's shunting equations (Lemma 6.4).

Proposition 6.3 (subtractive, derived). Shares tilt to large narratives, predicted totals depend on clustering granularity, it matches resource depletion only when κ is proportional to each narrative's own drive (which is division), and it makes every narrative Granger-cause every other by construction.

Conjecture 6.B · what "semi-conservative" now means
  • SC1 ω > 0 on held-out data, with B from predicted supply.
  • SC2 total supply mean-reverts once the number of ingested sources is controlled.
  • SC3 an outside shock lowers every tracked narrative by the same proportion, not the same amount.

Accept semi-conservation only if all three hold. If ω comes out near 0, that is a finding: newsroom supply behaves like excitation without a budget, and the competition in the literature belongs to readers.

Transition weights

Attribution shares, with the unexplained part sent to BOT

\[ \alpha_{j\to i,t} = \frac{\sigma(\eta_{i,t}/s)\sum_m A_{ij,m}\, z_{j,m,t}}{\lambda^{\text{raw}}_{i,t}}, \qquad p(\text{BOT}\to i) = 1 - \sum_j \alpha_{j\to i} \]

Lemmas 7.1 to 7.3 (derived): the shares are proper probabilities, divisive competition leaves them unchanged (subtraction would break them), and over a horizon they form a proper policy over parents.

Lemma 7.4 (derived): Hawkes coefficients measure offspring, not transfer. Attention on j that begets attention on i is not removed from j. Means cannot separate the two; the spread of the counts partly can. Transport-style flows are therefore only a cross-check.

One node, four jobs
  • The source of outside attention in the Hawkes model
  • The root of every explanation
  • The entry point for new information
  • The "we do not know" answer, priced at −log p0, so abstention competes with every story on equal terms
Search

The smallest explanation, with a certificate for each case

An explanation is an arborescence rooted at BOT that reaches every market event. Its cost is the sum of edge costs, and the smallest one is also the most probable one under independent edges, so competing explanations come with honest odds:

\[ c(u\to v) = -\log p(u\to v) + \lambda_{\text{node}}, \qquad S^{*} = \arg\min_{S} \sum_{e\in S} c(e), \qquad \frac{P(S_1)}{P(S_2)} = e^{\,\text{cost}(S_2) - \text{cost}(S_1)} \]
Theorem 12.6, derived · certified smallest explanation

Run push from every event with leftover mass \(R_x\), solve the explored subgraph exactly to get \(C_X\), and set

\[ \Gamma = \min_{x}\, \min\!\left(-\log\frac{R_x}{\alpha(1-\alpha)^{L}} + \lambda_{\text{node}},\; (L+1)\,\lambda_{\text{node}}\right) \]

If \(C_X \le \Gamma\), no explanation anywhere in the graph is cheaper. The proof was re-checked line by line and holds. When the check fails, Yggdrasil says so instead of claiming minimality.

Theorem 12.7, proven · Corollary 12.9, derived

Levin tree search with the transition weights as its policy expands at most

\[ N \le d_0(S)\cdot \exp\!\Big(\sum_{e\in S} -\log p(e)\Big) \]

nodes: effort grows with the surprisal of the true explanation. A better attention model directly means cheaper proofs. Mixing a little uniform probability into the policy keeps a finite bound even when the prior is badly wrong (Lemma 12.10), which PUCT cannot offer.

Exactness is affordable. Smallest connecting subgraphs are NP-hard and the directed and group variants approximate badly, but exact DP is exponential only in the number of events: \(O(3^k n + 2^k((k+\log n)n + m))\). For the NVIDIA cluster, k ≈ 6 and a 10,000-node local graph costs about 7 million basic steps.

Query budget. SerpApi queries go to whatever best separates the competing explanations, using EC2 with the corrected guarantee \((2\ln(1/p_{\min})+1)^2\), or \(1 - 1/e\) for a fixed budget. Retrieved pages count as pre-move evidence only if Yggdrasil had seen them before the cutoff.

Verdicts

Four yes/no checks across every consistent reading of the evidence

The evidence becomes a small logic program in which each report is assumed accurate unless a conflicting, copied, retracted or lower-tier report overrides it. Each consistent reading is a stable model. A solver asks, for explanation H, whether some or all readings support it, and whether some or all refute it:

SUPPORTEDEvery consistent reading supports H.
CONTRADICTEDEvery consistent reading refutes H.
UNRESOLVEDSome reading refutes H, but not all. Disconfirmation is surfaced, never hidden.
CONSISTENT-BUT-UNPROVENNo reading refutes H, and not every reading supports it.
  • Total and exclusive (Theorem 14.1, derived): every explanation gets exactly one verdict. If no consistent reading exists at all, the output is INCOHERENT-EVIDENCE, with a proof.
  • Cheap to decide (proven): "some reading" checks are NP-complete, "all readings" checks are coNP-complete, each verdict needs at most four SAT calls, and a linear-time grounded core backs every firm verdict.
  • Proven in Lean 4: the solver is untrusted. Its witness models and LRAT refutation proofs are checked in Lean, and #print axioms records whether the compiler was trusted for each verdict.
  • What Lean cannot do: it proves "given this program, the verdict is V". Whether the program reflects the world depends on extraction, copy detection and source tiers, which stay outside any proof.
