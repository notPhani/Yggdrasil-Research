§7 · Engine 3b · Evidence and verdicts · Session 6, locked (6.1–6.13′)

Put every story on trial, and prove the verdict

For a ten-year-old

Now every story goes on trial. For each one we search for one clue that would help it and two clues that would hurt it. Looking for what could prove you wrong is how good detectives avoid fooling themselves.

Every fact has to be copied word for word from a real page. Nobody is allowed to make things up. A logic judge then tries every way of believing the witnesses that doesn't contradict itself. If the story survives in all of them, it's SUPPORTED. If it dies in all of them, CONTRADICTED. If it dies in some, UNRESOLVED. If nothing touches it, CONSISTENT-BUT-UNPROVEN.

For one verdict, a math robot (Lean) checks the judge's logic line by line.

Corroboration (A6): occurred(E) needs a report of tier ≤ 2 or reports from two independent owners, and each burst's source concentration goes in the dossier, so a flood of fresh fake domains can't establish an event on its own. The signature check is the strongest part of the design. Half of every hypothesis is verified from Engine 2's own measurements (tier 0), with no text and no model. Queries come from the hypothesis's central claim: 1 confirming and 2 disconfirming (Heuer's rule: rank hypotheses by the evidence against them). That is at most 15 per case, all cached, so 250 free credits cover about 16 cases a month. \(P_{\text{pre}}\) candidates are date-bounded: tbs=cdr:1,cd_min:<τ*−14d>,cd_max:<τ*>.

Span grounding for every extractor (A5). A GKG amount or quotation becomes a tuple only if it matches a verbatim span on the fetched page, exactly like rule and verifier tuples. GDELT's own extraction errors therefore can't reach a verdict.

The verifier never generates. It answers yes, no or contradiction about spans that deterministic code has already found, so a tuple can't contain knowledge the page doesn't. With pinned weights, CPU inference and fixed threads, it is deterministic even before archiving, and a small encoder carries far less memorized hindsight than a chat model. Every verdict carries a verifier-dependent flag (verdict flips when all extractor = verifier tuples are removed). The replay also runs in deterministic-only mode, stages 1–2, side by side.

The decisive claim needs no model. The V3 technical report words its cost as "excluding the costs associated with prior research and ablation experiments". Subject V3, predicate cost_of, cue "excluding" gives scope = final_run_only, which is contrary to the all-in reading. So H3 can be CONTRADICTED by rules alone.

\[ \texttt{contrary}(t_1, t_2) \iff \text{subj}_1 = \text{subj}_2 \wedge \text{pred}_1 = \text{pred}_2 \wedge (\text{value}, \text{scope}, \text{polarity}) \text{ incompatible} \]

Contradiction is computed by rules, never by a model.

Tiers (frozen registry as of 2024-12-31): 0 = Yggdrasil's own measurements; 1 = primary (the actor, a filing, the paper, an official statement); 2 = wires and national outlets; 3 = local, aggregators, blogs, social, unknown. independent(R1, R2) holds iff the reports are in different copy groups (Engine 1) and have different owners. A copy is defeated outright, because copies add no independent support (Dong et al. 2009).

refuted(H)  :- schema_refuter(H, L), holds(L).

Proof · Theorem 14.1, the four verdicts partition every hypothesis derived

Lemma 14.2: every rule with head expl(H) contains not refuted(H). Every stable model is a model of the Clark completion, so expl(H) ∈ M forces some body true in M, hence refuted(H) ∉ M. No model contains both.

Exhaustive: if \(b_{\text{OUT}}\), the verdict is CONTRADICTED when \(c_{\text{OUT}}\) and UNRESOLVED otherwise. If not \(b_{\text{OUT}}\), it is SUPPORTED when \(c_{\text{IN}}\) and CONSISTENT-BUT-UNPROVEN otherwise. Exclusive: \(c_{\text{IN}}\) means every model has expl, so none has refuted (14.2). That gives ¬\(b_{\text{OUT}}\), which rules out UNRESOLVED and CONTRADICTED. \(c_{\text{OUT}}\) with a non-empty model set gives \(b_{\text{OUT}}\), and no model has expl, so ¬\(c_{\text{IN}}\). UNRESOLVED gives a model with refuted, hence without expl, so ¬\(c_{\text{IN}}\). CONSISTENT-BUT-UNPROVEN requires ¬\(b_{\text{OUT}}\), which implies ¬\(c_{\text{OUT}}\) when models exist. QED

Deterministic: brave and cautious consequences are the union and intersection over the set of stable models. That is a unique set, independent of the order in which the solver finds models. Tight: the positive dependency graph (holds → ok, refuted → holds, expl → holds) has no cycle, because ok depends on defeated only through negation. So Fages' theorem applies: stable models are exactly the models of the completion. Complexity: brave reasoning is NP-complete and cautious is coNP-complete for normal programs. The verdict is a fixed Boolean combination of at most five SAT calls, which puts it in \(\Theta_2^P\). Disjunctive rules would raise it to \(\Sigma_2^P/\Pi_2^P\), so the rule language forbids them.

Proof · Theorem 14.4, the locked template always has a stable model derived E1: 0 / 3,000

Retracted reports and copies are defeated unconditionally, so they drop out. Among the remaining reports, R2 defeats R exactly when their literals are contrary, they are independent, and \(\text{tier}(R2) \le \text{tier}(R)\). contrary is symmetrized and independent is symmetric (different owners), and tiers form a total preorder. So an attack goes both ways between equal tiers, and only from better to worse between different tiers. Build a set S tier by tier, best tier first: add a report unless it conflicts with one already in S. Every report left out conflicts with a member of S of equal or better tier, which defeats it. Members of S never conflict with each other. So S is a stable extension, and with the standard ok/defeated encoding it gives a stable model of the program. The layers holds, refuted and expl sit above it and are stratified. QED

Consequences. INCOHERENT-EVIDENCE cannot occur under the locked template. Adversaries can force UNRESOLVED only by putting up sources of equal or better tier than the truth, never a false SUPPORTED or CONTRADICTED. Generated programs can still add bridge rules (\(\texttt{holds}(X) \leftarrow \texttt{holds}(Y)\)), and those can form positive loops that break tightness. So A4 checks every ground program for cycles through positive dependencies before the Lean path. A non-tight program skips Lean, and its clingo verdicts still stand. E1's control confirms the check catches such a loop.

When there is no consistent reading at all (unreachable under the locked template, Theorem 14.4)

This is kept for future rule types (undercutters, asymmetric preferences). Odd attack cycles can leave no stable model. This is geometric frustration: an antiferromagnetic triangle has no ground state that satisfies every bond. Yggdrasil then emits INCOHERENT-EVIDENCE, with an UNSAT proof that no model exists, and falls back to the grounded labelling, the least fixpoint of the defense operator. It is unique, polynomial, and equal to the well-founded model. A true core atom gives SUPPORTED or CONTRADICTED. An undefined atom with arguments on both sides gives UNRESOLVED. Anything else is CONSISTENT-BUT-UNPROVEN. How often real evidence is incoherent is unknown, and the dossier counts it.

Game theory · the verdict as a debate

Grounded acceptance has an exact game reading (Modgil & Caminada 2009). A proponent claims an argument. An opponent attacks it, the proponent must counter-attack every attack, and the proponent may not repeat an argument. The argument is in the grounded extension iff the proponent has a winning strategy. Equal-tier mutual attacks are the two-player standoff where neither side can force a win, which is why they land in UNRESOLVED. The stable models are the two self-consistent "camps", much like the two pure equilibria of a coordination game (that last part is an analogy, not a theorem). Diagnosticity, the evidence items whose removal flips a verdict (n + 1 solver runs), is the debate's list of decisive moves, and it is Heuer's sensitivity analysis made mechanical.

What Lean proves: given ground program P (with this hash), the verdict for H is V. What it does not: that the extraction was right, that the copy detection was right, that the tiers are right, or that the attention model proposed the right stories. Every published verdict states that boundary. LRAT checking is linear-time (Cruz-Filipe et al. 2017). A garbled certificate can only fail the check, never produce a false theorem.

The dossier design · 6.8

  • The event, clusters and τ*; for the best explanation, each rival, and "we do not know": the tree, each edge's probability, odds against the best, the placebo p-value, and hub flags.
  • Per hypothesis, two verdicts: before the cutoff (\(P_{\text{pre}}\)) and at report time (\(P_{\text{all}}\)), shown separately.
  • Behind each verdict: source, tier, first-seen time, the quoted span, the link, the contradictions found, the diagnosticity list, and what would flip it.
  • The verifier-dependent flag, and the deterministic-only comparison.
  • Abstention is worded "no admissible explanation in observed English news before τ*", never "no cause" (A8).
  • No forecast and no advice, anywhere. Kept forever with every cited item.
