Events, narratives, phenomena: one rule at three timescales

For a ten-year-old

Now we sort the stapled bundles. Small piles are single happenings: "Nvidia fell 14% before the bell". Boxes hold ongoing stories: "cheap Chinese AI". Big shelves hold whole eras: "the AI boom".

Every box keeps its name forever. If a box gets lumpy, it splits into two boxes and they share its memory fairly. If a box gathers dust for a week, it goes to sleep, and it needs a really good match to wake up again. The sorter only looks at what an article says, never at how popular it is. Popularity is the next engine's job.

Features design · 3.2

\[ e_o = \text{title embedding (384-d, int8, reused from dedup)}, \qquad \text{ents}(o) = \operatorname{clean}(\text{persons} \cup \text{orgs} \cup \text{allnames}) \]
\[ \operatorname{idf}(x) = \log \frac{N_{\text{docs}}}{\operatorname{df}(x)} \ \ \text{(trailing 7 days)} \]
\[ J_{\text{idf}}(A, B) = \frac{\sum_{x \in A \cap B} \operatorname{idf}(x)}{\sum_{x \in A \cup B} \operatorname{idf}(x)} \qquad \text{(weighted Jaccard; definition written for this doc)} \]

Themes enter only at the narrative level, and only those with document frequency below 20%. The measured top themes are generic tags: TAX_FNCACT 77%, EPU_POLICY 39%, CRISISLEX_CRISISLEXREC 33% measured. clean lowercases, applies an alias map and drops known misfiles. "los angeles" was tagged as a person in 26 documents of one batch.

Layer 1: event clusters (one specific happening)

\[ \operatorname{score}(o, c) = \big( w_{\text{txt}} \cos(e_o, \mu_c) + w_{\text{ent}} J_{\text{idf}}(\text{ents}_o, \text{ents}_c) \big) \cdot k_{\text{event}}(t_o - t_c), \qquad k_\ell(\Delta) = e^{-\Delta^2 / 2\sigma_\ell^2},\ \sigma_{\text{event}} = 72\,\text{h} \]
\[ \operatorname{assign}(o) = \begin{cases} \arg\max_c \operatorname{score}(o, c) & \text{if } \max_c \operatorname{score}(o,c) \ge \tau_{\text{event}} \\ \text{new cluster} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Candidates are clusters seen in the last 7 days that share a rare entity or an embedding LSH bucket with o. \(\mu_c\) is the running mean of member embeddings, and \(t_c\) the mean member time. Objects are processed one at a time in D4 order, so the result is deterministic. One measured batch clustered alone gave 61–67% singletons: events don't repeat within 15 minutes, they build up over hours and days measured.

Layer 2: persistent narratives, assignment from content only design · 3.9′

\[ \operatorname{gain}_t(c, n) = \underbrace{\log f(x_c \mid n) - \log f_0(x_c)}_{\text{learned likelihood ratio (3.4′)}} \; - \underbrace{\frac{(t_c - t_n)^2}{2\sigma_{\text{narr}}^2}}_{\log k_{\text{narr}},\ \sigma_{\text{narr}} = 14\,\text{d}} \]
\[ c \to \begin{cases} \arg\max_{n\ \text{alive}} \operatorname{gain} & \text{if } \ge \tau_{\text{alive}} \\ \arg\max_{n\ \text{dormant}} \operatorname{gain} & \text{if } \ge \tau_{\text{dormant}} \gt \tau_{\text{alive}} \\ \text{emerge new } n,\ \text{id} = \operatorname{sha256}(\text{first event ids}, t) & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad \mu_n \leftarrow \frac{(1-\eta)\mu_n + \eta\,\mu_c}{\lVert\cdot\rVert} \]

\(t_n\) is when narrative n last changed. That is Engine 2a's own state, not attention. The centroid drifts slowly (small η) while the id never changes. Waking a dormant narrative takes stronger evidence than joining a live one. That is hysteresis, the same reason a thermostat has a dead band.

Learned calibration: the softmax is a mixture posterior design · 3.4′

\[ f(x \mid n) = C_d(\kappa)\, e^{\kappa\, \mu_n^{\top} x}, \quad C_d(\kappa) = \frac{\kappa^{d/2 - 1}}{(2\pi)^{d/2} I_{d/2-1}(\kappa)}, \qquad f_0(x) = \frac{1}{A_d},\ A_d = \frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)} \]
\[ \log \frac{p(n \mid x)}{p(0 \mid x)} = \kappa \cos(x, \mu_n) + \log \pi_n + \log C_d(\kappa) + w_{\text{ent}} J_{\text{idf}} + w_{\text{th}} J_{\text{th}} - \log \pi_0 + \log A_d^{-1} \]

Title embeddings live on the unit sphere. Each narrative is a von Mises–Fisher bump around its centroid, and "none" is a flat background over the whole sphere. The temperature of the softmax is therefore \(T = 1/\kappa\), and the "none" threshold is fixed by \(\pi_0\) and \(\kappa\). Both are fitted by EM on the data itself, with no hand labels:

\[ \text{E: } r_{cn} = \frac{\pi_n f(x_c \mid n)\, e^{w \cdot J}}{\sum_{n'} \pi_{n'} f(x_c \mid n')\, e^{w \cdot J}} \qquad \text{M: } \mu_n = \frac{\sum_c r_{cn} x_c}{\lVert \sum_c r_{cn} x_c \rVert},\ \ \bar r = \frac{\sum_n \lVert \sum_c r_{cn} x_c \rVert}{\sum_{n,c} r_{cn}},\ \ \hat\kappa = \frac{\bar r (d - \bar r^2)}{1 - \bar r^2},\ \ \pi_n = \overline{r_{\cdot n}} \]

Given the responsibilities, the entity and theme weights solve a convex weighted logistic regression. The refit runs daily on the trailing 7 days with a fixed initialization and iteration count, versioned as theta_version. \(\hat\kappa\) is Banerjee et al.'s approximation, and \(\log C_d\) needs a log-Bessel routine at d = 384. Calibration is checked against where each cluster has settled 72 hours later, as a reliability curve. That check is a diagnostic only, never a training signal, because training on its own settled output would teach the model to agree with itself.

Sensitivity worth knowing · κ is large in 384 dimensions derived arithmetic

\(\hat\kappa\) is about \(d \bar r / (1 - \bar r^2)\). For mean resultant lengths \(\bar r\) = 0.3, 0.5, 0.8 it gives κ ≈ 127, 256, 852. A cosine gap of only 0.01 between two narratives is then worth 1.3–8.5 nats, i.e. odds of 3.6:1 to 5,000:1. In practice, soft membership will be close to hard except at near-ties. Three things follow. The top-3 slots matter mainly at boundaries. The "none" component, not the softmax spread, carries most of the uncertainty. And the entity and theme terms are what decide close calls. The real \(\bar r\) is unmeasured until the first run.

Soft membership and the series Engine 2b consumes design · 3.3

\[ p_{c,n} = r_{cn}\ \text{for}\ n \in \text{top}_3(c) \cup \{0\}, \quad p_{o,n} = p_{c(o),n}, \quad \tilde p = \tfrac{1}{255}\operatorname{round}_{\text{LR}}(255\,p) \]
\[ y_{n,t} = \sum_{o \in t} \tilde p_{o,n}, \qquad m_{n,t} = \sum_{o \in t} \text{member_count}(o)\, \tilde p_{o,n} \]

Memberships are decided once per event cluster. Five DeepSeek documents in one batch share a single decision instead of making five noisy ones. They are stored as uint8 and quantized before aggregation, so replay and live give identical \(y\). \(y\) is the unweighted attention mass. \(m\) is reach, which uses Engine 1's member counts as data. proposed detail \(\operatorname{round}_{\text{LR}}\) is largest-remainder rounding, which makes the four uint8 values sum to exactly 255. Independent rounding can sum to 254 or 256 and would break Lemma 5.1 by up to 1/255 per object.

Proof · Lemma 5.1, the reservoir identity derived

For every window, \(\sum_{n \ge 1} y_{n,t} + y_{0,t} = V_t\), the number of objects. Each object's memberships over its top 3 plus "none" sum to 1 (to 255/255 exactly under largest-remainder rounding), and every other narrative gets 0. Summing over objects gives \(\sum_o 1 = V_t\). QED Consequence: total attention is conserved trivially by construction, so "semi-conservation" can't be tested on the total. It must be tested as competition for predicted supply, which is \(\omega\) in Engine 2b.

Proof · Lemma 5.2, memory is conserved under splits and merges derived

Split \(N \to c_1..c_K\) with overlap shares \(q_c = |\text{obs}(c) \cap \text{obs}(N)| / \sum_{c'} |\text{obs}(c') \cap \text{obs}(N)|\), and set \(z_{c,m} = q_c z_{N,m}\). Then \(\sum_c z_{c,m} = z_{N,m} \sum_c q_c = z_{N,m}\) for every timescale m. A merge sets \(z_{C,m} = \sum_l z_{N_l,m}\), which is conserved by definition. QED Lemma 8.3 extends this to the parameters: at the split instant, every outside narrative's drive is unchanged and the children's drives sum to the parent's.

Layer 3: phenomena (the slow field) design · 3.10 with 3.9′

\[ W_{ij} = a \cos(\mu_i, \mu_j) + b\, J_{\text{idf}}(E_i, E_j) + d\, \operatorname{comem}(i, j), \qquad \text{phenomena} = \operatorname{Leiden}(W),\ \text{fixed seed, daily} \]
\[ \Phi_p(t) = \sum_{i \in p} y_i(t), \qquad z^{\Phi}_{p,m}(t) = \sum_{i \in p} z_{i,m}(t) \ \text{(exact, because the trace recursion is linear)} \]

There are no Engine 2b inputs in \(W\). The mutual-excitation term was removed when you ruled the attention coupling out of scope. A phenomenon's identity changes only after 7 consecutive daily checks agree. That is far stiffer than the narrative layer's three checks every six hours. Two different kernels are involved here and they shouldn't be confused. Phenomena have no freshness kernel for assignment (they are persistent). Their attention \(\Phi\) is remembered with a 30-day memory kernel. Phenomena are labels in Engine 3, not search nodes, because as nodes they would double-count their members' attention.

Why they exist: they form a slow baseline under the narratives, \(\beta_i \log \Phi_{p(i)}(t-1)\) in Engine 2b's drive. Without it, a months-long boom gets read as narratives exciting each other. That is the Filimonov–Sornette spurious-criticality trap at a slow timescale. The physics is a Born–Oppenheimer separation: phenomena are the slow nuclei, and narratives are fast electrons that settle into whatever state the phenomenon is in. Cut note: the slow-baseline coupling is deferred for the hackathon, because three weeks of history can't show a multi-year phenomenon. Phenomena ship as dossier labels.

History for phenomena: the anytime download proposed

\[ \pi_b(\ell) = \min\!\Big(1,\ n_\ell\, \frac{\text{size}_b}{\sum_{b' \in \text{day}} \text{size}_{b'}}\Big), \qquad u_b = \operatorname{hash}(\text{batch_ts}) \in [0,1), \qquad b \in s_\ell \iff u_b \lt \pi_b(\ell) \]
\[ \hat\Phi_p(d) = \sum_{b \in s_\ell} \frac{y_{p,b}}{\pi_b(\ell)}, \qquad \mathbb E\big[\hat\Phi_p(d)\big] = \sum_b \Pr(b \in s_\ell)\,\frac{y_{p,b}}{\pi_b(\ell)} = \sum_b y_{p,b} = \Phi_p(d) \]
Proof · why stopping anywhere is safe Horvitz–Thompson, permanent random numbers

GDELT files are all-or-nothing per 15-minute batch, so "weight by popularity" means sampling batches with probability proportional to file size. That is PPS sampling, and the manifest gives the sizes for free. Unbiased: the display line above is the one-line Horvitz–Thompson argument, using \(\Pr(b \in s_\ell) = \pi_b(\ell)\) when \(u_b\) is uniform. Nested: if \(\pi_b(\ell) \le \pi_b(\ell+1)\), then \(u_b \lt \pi_b(\ell)\) implies \(u_b \lt \pi_b(\ell + 1)\), so \(s_\ell \subseteq s_{\ell+1}\) and each pass only adds batches. Anytime: within pass ℓ, batches download in increasing \(u_b\). Stopping when the next \(u_b\) reaches \(u^*\) leaves exactly the batches with \(u_b \lt \pi'_b = \max\big(\pi_b(\ell-1), \min(\pi_b(\ell), u^*)\big)\). That is again a Poisson sample with known inclusion probabilities, so the Horvitz–Thompson weights \(1/\pi'_b\) stay exact. National statistics offices use the same trick (Brewer; Ohlsson) to coordinate surveys. QED
